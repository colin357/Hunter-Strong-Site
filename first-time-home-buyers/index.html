First-Time Home Buyer Loans

How much do I need to buy my first home?

Less than most people think. Here's the general range by loan type:

  • Conventional: as little as 3% down for qualifying first-time buyers. Mortgage insurance (PMI) can come off once you reach 20% equity.
  • FHA: 3.5% down with a credit score of 580 or higher. FHA is more forgiving of lower scores and higher debt-to-income ratios.
  • VA: $0 down for eligible veterans, service members and some surviving spouses, with no monthly mortgage insurance.

On top of the down payment you'll have closing costs and prepaid items (taxes and insurance), so plan your cash to close rather than only the down payment. Gift money from family and seller-paid closing costs can cover a lot of it.

What credit score do I need?

As a rough guide: around 580 for FHA at 3.5% down, and around 620 for most conventional loans. VA has no VA-set minimum, but lenders set their own. If your score isn't there yet, Hunter can show you what to pay down or fix, and roughly how long it may take. He'd rather work on a plan with you than leave you with a "no."

Is there down payment assistance?

Yes. State housing finance agencies run assistance programs for qualified buyers: ADFA in Arkansas, Mississippi Home Corporation in Mississippi and THDA in Tennessee. Income, price and credit limits apply and programs change, so ask Hunter which ones you may qualify for today.

The first-time buyer roadmap

  1. Get pre-approved. You'll know your price range and payment before you shop.
  2. Pick a payment you're comfortable with, not just the maximum you're approved for.
  3. Find a home with your real estate agent. Hunter can run numbers on any listing you send him.
  4. Go under contract and lock your rate.
  5. Appraisal, underwriting and conditions. Hunter's team works to keep you updated the whole way.
  6. Close and get your keys.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy a house with no money down?

Yes, if you qualify for a VA loan, which allows $0 down for eligible veterans and service members. Otherwise, down payment assistance programs, gift funds from family and seller-paid closing costs can bring your out-of-pocket cost close to zero on a conventional or FHA loan.

Should I rent or buy?

It depends on how long you plan to stay and what a mortgage payment looks like next to your rent. Hunter can run a side-by-side comparison of your rent and an estimated total house payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance and mortgage insurance) so you can decide with real numbers.

How long does pre-approval take?

Often the same day once Hunter has your application, income documents and credit. Applying through his secure online application is the fastest way to start.

Does getting pre-approved hurt my credit?

A mortgage credit check is a hard inquiry, which can move your score a few points. Credit scoring models generally treat multiple mortgage inquiries within a short window as one, so you can shop without being penalized for each check.
