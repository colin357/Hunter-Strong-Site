Refinancing Your Mortgage

The short answer

Refinancing replaces your current mortgage with a new one to lower your payment, shorten your term, remove mortgage insurance or take cash out of your equity. Hunter Strong (NMLS #1911747, FirstTrust Home Loans) runs a break-even analysis for homeowners in Arkansas, Mississippi, Tennessee and 10 other states so you can see whether a refinance actually saves money after closing costs.

Types of refinance

  • Rate-and-term: change your rate, your term (for example, 30 to 15 years) or both.
  • Cash-out: borrow against your equity for renovations, debt consolidation or other big expenses.
  • FHA streamline: a simplified refinance for existing FHA loans.
  • VA IRRRL: a streamlined rate reduction for existing VA loans.
  • FHA to conventional: often used to remove FHA mortgage insurance once you have enough equity.

When does refinancing make sense?

Divide your total closing costs by your monthly savings. That's your break-even point in months. If you'll stay in the home longer than that, the refinance usually makes sense. Hunter also checks the total interest over the life of the loan, so a lower payment doesn't quietly cost you more in the long run.

Frequently asked questions

How much equity do I need to do a cash-out refinance?

Conventional cash-out refinances generally let you borrow up to 80% of your home's value, so you keep at least 20% equity. VA cash-out can go higher for eligible borrowers.

How soon can I refinance after buying?

It depends on the loan type. Some refinances can be done within months, while others have seasoning requirements of six months or more. Ask Hunter about your specific loan.

Will refinancing reset my loan term?

Usually yes, unless you choose a shorter term. Hunter can show you options, such as a 20- or 15-year term, that keep you on track to pay off the house on schedule.
